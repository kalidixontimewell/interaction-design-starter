History Of D&D

Dungeons & Dragons (D&D) is a game created by Ernest Gary Gygax and David Arneson. By 1997, the game had been acquired by Wizards Of The Coast. In 1971, Arneson adapted the Chainmail game rules for a fantasy campaign. This was set in his fictional world of “Blackmoor” therefore creating the first D&D game to use elements like hit points and experience points. In 1973, Gygax and Arneson started to solidify their ideas and co-write the game rules for D&D. By 1974, the first edition of D&D was published under Tactical Studies Rules (TSR) When originally released, D&D was initially met with confusion as it strayed away from traditional board games, due to its open-ended storytelling. Over time, it gained a loyal following and prompted a rapid growth of the D&D community.

How It Works

D&D is often set in a medieval fantasy world. There are usually 3-6 players per session, alongside the Dungeon Master (DM). The DM makes up the story as you progress, and you act out your character using the dice to dictate decisions, such as how much damage the villain takes or your character takes. You create your character using a character sheet or using websites online (e.g. D&D Beyond).
